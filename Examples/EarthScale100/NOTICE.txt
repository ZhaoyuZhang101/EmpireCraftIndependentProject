The player Earth's geographic water reference uses the Ocean and Lakes polygons
from Natural Earth v5.1.2 at 1:10 million scale, in the public domain. The global
marine polygon supplies tidal-water connectivity. The disconnected inland
polygon and mapped lakes supply lake outlines. Reference levels are estimated
from shoreline elevations on the project's ETOPO overview. Narrow straits may
provide connectivity beneath exposed coarse cells. The original ETOPO grids
contain area-averaged elevations. In new Earth worlds, a cell whose centre and
more than half its surface lie in a mapped major lake can use the lake level as
its representative bed when the surrounding banks have raised the cell mean.
Where ETOPO depicts an extensive flat water surface, the bed can also rise by
up to two metres to agree with the shoreline estimate. These exceptions add no
assumed depth. Their physical resolution still depends on the chosen map size.
https://www.naturalearthdata.com/about/terms-of-use/

python Tools/EarthScale100/bake_player_water.py --check reconstructs the six
EarthWater resources from SHA-256-verified source geometry using NumPy, Pillow
and Shapely. OWR2 retains the OWR1 signed water-level spans and appends a sparse
index of representative beds. OWR3 additionally stores the index and reference
surface elevation for supplemental lakes that cover a majority of a previously
dry cell. OWR4 adds a sorted list of cells along selected straits. Each route
crosses shared wet cell faces inside a single connected part of the source
ocean polygon between its two mouths. The bake minimizes the number of changed
cells. Where the original grid bed is at or above sea level a representative
bed of -1 metre encodes unresolved water depth. V5 Earth saves use these strait
beds. V4 saves retain their lake-aware baseline and V1–V3 retain the raw DEM.
Raw geometry downloads are cached under ValidationOutput/BasinWater.

EarthStraitShore.bytes uses both banks and enclosed islands of the Natural
Earth v5.1.2 1:10 million Ocean polygon (public domain). It covers 18 straits
and their approaches, coastal river endpoints, and 14 wider river-mouth or
estuary regions. These include the Nile delta, Pearl delta, Yangtze estuary,
Hangzhou Bay, Meghna estuary and Amazon mouth. Regions blend into the gameplay
coast at their margins. The strait shore samples are 217 m apart north to south.
Estuary samples are 434 m apart. Source accuracy is limited by the original
cartography. New Earth maps and V5 saves use this regional coast. Older saves
retain their own shoreline baseline.

ORBIS clips the native cropland class to the public-domain Natural Earth
v5.1.2 Nile Delta landform. It aggregates crop area on a 0.05-degree grid
and quantizes fractions in fifteenths. Ground cover and root-zone moisture
are ecological representations of these fields. The game keeps its own
precipitation, water levels, water budgets and stored climate classifications.

EarthRivers.bytes uses the public-domain Natural Earth v5.1.2 Rivers + Lake
Centerlines at 1:10 million. The player atlas includes 705 drawable parts.
The Yangtze changes from a river line into the Ocean polygon at Zhenjiang. Its
chart centreline follows connected water pixels between the mapped banks and
islands to the mouth identified by the 1:110 million Chang river endpoint.
Selection uses scale rank up to 5 and minimum zoom up to 4.7, with named
regional rivers and connecting reaches listed in the bake script. The bake
preserves multipart boundaries and shared confluences. Generalized courses
are simplified at about 145 m tolerance in a local ground metric before
registration to the available transects. ORV2 encodes normalized
24-bit coordinates and full width in metres at 77,696 vertices. Encoding
precision does not increase the positional accuracy of the source cartography.
Water depth and animation are illustrative. ETOPO endpoint elevations orient
the optical current where the source line was digitized upstream. Charted
tidal extensions run from the upstream survey toward their marine receiver.
At player zoom, turns resolvable on screen are rounded within the surveyed
channel. The original station remains inside both banks. Confluences and
terminal positions are fixed. Land, river-map ink and the vegetation test
use the same channel geometry.
Rainfall, runoff and gameplay drainage continue to use the simulated catchments.

Allen, G. H. and Pavelsky, T. M. Global River Widths from Landsat (GRWL),
summary statistics V01.02 and vector V01.01, CC BY 4.0. ORBIS matches the
surveyed courses to nearby parallel Landsat reaches. In 13 tiles covering the
Nile delta and selected Chinese and Indian rivers, it uses local medians of
13 adjacent 30 m transects. In other regions it uses the observed reach median
where available. Short gaps interpolate along the same river. In long gaps
the width tends toward the typical matched width for the same map prominence.
Widths are optical estimates near the satellite product's reference discharge.
They are not measurements of river flow. Within continuous, identifiable
GRWL reaches the baked centreline follows ordered Landsat transects. Shared
junctions and mapped outlets are pinned. Ambiguous arms and observation gaps
retain the Natural Earth course. Player attribution is in EarthWaterCredits.txt.

The Huangpu mainstem follows the Shanghai Water Authority's alignment from
the Dianshan Lake outlet to Wusongkou on the Yangtze. Its upper 32.5 km
use ESA WorldCover 2021 v200 tile N30E120, class 80 (permanent water).
ORBIS follows the channel between forks, spans short gaps in the water mask
and estimates widths from the opposite banks on the native 10 m raster.
The remainder uses GRWL V01.01 tile NH51, segment 5, stations 2550 down to
83. Ordered transects supply the centreline and local widths. Missing
observations borrow nearby widths from the same channel. The resulting
113.9 km line stops in the surveyed Yangtze water at Wusongkou.
WorldCover attribution and licence are given above and in EarthWaterCredits.txt.
https://swj.sh.gov.cn/shsdfbzssglc-tpxw/20201009/f5c0d47bcde1426fb03fdf93a9aabbda.html

EarthMajorLakes.bytes uses the Natural Earth lakes, the enclosed Caspian
polygon and four HydroLAKES shorelines. The 8192 by 4096 atlas encodes signed great-circle distances from
the mapped shores, including islands. Selection uses mapped spherical area
and the regional landmarks documented in EU4-Lakes.md. EarthLakeDetail.bytes
packs 3,082 near-shore pages of the same distance field at eight times the
overview density. Shorelines describe the published dataset, which includes
reservoirs and variable lakes. python Tools/EarthScale100/bake_major_lakes.py
--check reconstructs both shoreline textures for comparison.

Four additional lakes use HydroLAKES v1.0, by Messager, M. L., Lehner, B.,
Grill, G., Nedeva, I. and Schmitt, O. (2016), licensed under CC BY 4.0.
We selected Hylak_id 1295 (Sayram), 1479 (Erhai), 1483 (Dianchi) and 1485
(Fuxian) from the original HydroLAKES_polys_v10 shapefile. The shipped subset
in global/china-lakes-hydrolakes-v1.geojson keeps the WGS84 rings, including
islands, rounded to six decimal places, and the source Elevation in metres.
ORBIS rasterizes the outlines and uses that elevation as a reference level for
majority-water cells. Player distributions include EarthWaterCredits.txt.

The 100 m grid is derived from the 1 arc-second GLO-30 digital surface model and the 15 arc-second ETOPO surface elevation and seafloor grid. Heights are rounded to whole real metres relative to EGM2008. The metadata lists the exact input files and SHA-256 checksums. Cropping, interpolation, projection and combination were performed by the ORBIS test-map generator.
